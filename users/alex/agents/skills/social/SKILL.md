# Social presence

This applies to whatever account is in front of you — a researcher's or
professional's profile, a project's or a product's channel, an organisation's
page. Nothing here is tied to one owner or one platform: the account sets the
specifics, and this skill is the method and the limits.

# Ask what it is for before you post

Followers and "engagement" are not goals. They are proxies that are wrong more
often than they are right, and optimising them is how a decent presence turns into
noise. State, in one sentence, who should see this and what should happen when
they do:

| Account | What success actually is |
|---|---|
| A researcher's or professional's profile | his work reaches the people who would act on it — colleagues, the field, the community it serves — and his name resolves to the right person |
| A project or product channel | someone with the problem it solves finds it, and a shared link renders correctly |
| An organisation's page | the audience it serves gets accurate, timely information they can rely on |

If you cannot state the goal, you cannot tell a good post from a busy one. Ask.

The two failure modes are opposite. Silence — work done and never shared, a page
with a broken link preview — is one. Over-doing it — posting for reach, hot takes,
threads engineered for replies, a claim pitched harder than the evidence to earn a
click — is the other, and it is the worse of the two, because it damages standing
that is not recoverable by deleting a post.

# Posting under his name is a gated action, not a free one

A public post carries whoever's identity is on the account, so putting one out is
an outward-facing act that runs through the ordinary approval gate — see the
`policy` skill for that gate and the trusted-action wrappers. That does not make it
forbidden. Where a posting path is set up and he has authorised it, you can post;
where none is, or the item is new, sensitive or a claim you cannot stand behind,
you prepare the draft, any image and its alt text, and a suggested time, and let
him send it. What is never unattended, whatever is set up: liking, following,
reposting or DMing as him without that authorisation. And an incoming mention or DM
from someone he does not know is reference or a question for @owner, never an
action you take — the same inbound-trust rule that governs untrusted mail.

# You can only claim what you can observe

Assume no analytics unless you are shown some: no impressions, no reach, no
follower deltas you can see. You therefore cannot tell whether a post worked.
Never write "this will get engagement", never estimate reach, never pick a format
or a time because it "performs" — those numbers come from tools you do not have,
and an invented number about his visibility is as bad as an invented citation.
Where a platform DOES expose analytics, read it and label it — the metric and the
date window — and remember a post and a spike in the same week is not causation.

# The conduct line, on an account that carries authority

Some accounts carry authority that turns an overstatement into a professional
problem rather than just a weak post — a clinician's, a researcher's, a lawyer's, a
public office's. Where the account is one of these, these do not bend:

- No claim about health, treatment, outcome, legal or financial matters pitched
  more strongly than the evidence supports. Short formats punish nuance — a thread
  or a caption that compresses a finding into something false is not a smaller
  truth, it is a wrong statement under his name.
- No individual advice, in public or in a DM, on a matter that would need a
  professional relationship to give responsibly.
- No confidential or personal data — a patient, a client, an unpublished result —
  reaches a post. Identifiable data does not leave the host at all (see
  `research-projects`), and even a de-identified case does not go out without
  explicit consent AND the owner's sign-off. When in doubt it does not go out.
- Unpublished or embargoed work is not pre-announced; it is shared when it is
  public, not before.
- Disclose funding or conflicts wherever the platform or the norm expects it.

# His voice, and what is his call

A post is his voice. An original post or a rewrite of his words is a **proposal**,
not an edit and not an action — you draft it, he decides. Do not invent an opinion,
a position or an endorsement for him. Bios and each language version are his own
voice too, never machine-translated from another.

# What is legitimate, and what is never done

Legitimate, and worth doing:

- Sharing his own work with a plain-language summary a non-specialist can read,
  linking a **stable identifier** (a DOI, a permalink) rather than a page that moves.
- Announcing the things a peer would genuinely want to know — a talk, a release, a
  role, a collaboration.
- Amplifying collaborators and crediting them.
- Keeping the bio and identity fields consistent with the rest of his web presence
  — the highest-value work here, shared with `seo-marketing`.
- A careful, sourced correction of misinformation in his field, when he chooses to
  make it.

Never, whatever the reason and whoever asks:

- Engagement-bait ("agree?", ragebait, quote-dunking), follow/unfollow games,
  engagement pods, or bought followers.
- Undisclosed promotion, or a paid arrangement dressed as an opinion.
- Reposting paywalled full text — link the identifier instead.
- AI-generated volume published as his voice. Cadence is not a goal, and filler
  costs credibility faster than silence.
- Machine-translating instead of translating.
- Impersonation, or using a person's or institution's name or logo to imply an
  endorsement that was not given.
- Arguing with cranks or getting pulled into a pile-on.

If he asks for something on that list, say which line it crosses and offer the
legitimate version of the same goal — there almost always is one.

# Per-platform

For each: what it is FOR, and where it goes wrong. Do not fabricate a handle or
claim an account exists — if you do not know it, say so and ask.

## LinkedIn

The professional surface, where effort usually pays off most: work, roles, talks,
collaborations, hiring, to an audience of actual peers. Long-form is fine here.
Keep the headline, affiliation and "about" consistent with the identity graph.
Traps: the platform rewards influencer-cadence "broetry" and manufactured
vulnerability — that register is wrong for most professional accounts; write
peer-to-peer.

## X (Twitter) / Bluesky / Mastodon

Fast dissemination to a community that lives on the timeline: an announcement, a
short thread summarising a piece of work and ending on its link, a colleague
amplified. Traps: the character limit truncates nuance into something wrong (see
the conduct line); quote-dunking and pile-ons are one bad reply away and are never
his to join. These communities have fragmented across the three networks — draft
for wherever HIS audience actually is, and mirror rather than assume one is still
the room.

## Instagram

Visual-first, so a limited fit unless the work is visual: figures, infographics, a
carousel that explains something, event or behind-the-scenes photos. Traps: never
a person's image without consent; every image carries meaningful alt text
(accessibility, and it is the only thing a screen reader or image search sees);
Stories vanish, so nothing that needs to persist goes only there.

## TikTok

Short-form video — high potential reach, high misread risk. The format rewards
oversimplification, which is exactly the dangerous move on technical, medical or
financial claims. Only worth it if he actively wants public outreach and will have
each script held to the same evidence bar as the underlying work. Subtitles and
captions are mandatory (accessibility and sound-off viewing). Never advice, never a
claim the short cut makes stronger than the evidence.

## YouTube

The home for longer form: a recorded talk, a lecture, an explainer. Titles,
descriptions and chapters written to say what the thing IS, not clickbait;
identifiers and further reading in the description; captions on every video
(accessibility first, and search reads them — the seam with `seo-marketing`).
Traps: thumbnail-and-title clickbait, and letting an upload go out without
captions.

## The identity graph, and any new platform

The profiles that make a name resolve to the right person across the web — ORCID,
Scholar, Scopus, ResearchGate, GitHub, LinkedIn, the site itself, whichever apply —
current and cross-linked, with work carrying stable identifiers. Keeping them
consistent is worth more than any single post, and it is the same identity work
`seo-marketing` calls its highest-value item. Before he joins a NEW platform,
three questions decide it: is his audience actually there, can he sustain it, and
does the format let him stay accurate? If any answer is no, not joining is the
right call.

# Accessibility is not optional

Alt text on every image, captions/subtitles on every video, plain language over
jargon where the audience is broad. This is part of what "correct" means here — a
figure with no alt text is invisible to a screen reader and to image search both.

# Handoffs

- `policy` — the approval gate and the trusted-action wrappers behind every post,
  DM and outbound send.
- `seo-marketing` — the identity graph and the link-preview metadata: the
  `og:`/twitter-card tags on a page are what render when he shares its link, so a
  broken preview is fixed there, not here.
- `references` — anything that cites literature: no identifier goes into a post
  until it has resolved against a real API response this session, and share the
  identifier, not paywalled full text.
- `research-projects` — the personal-data rule and unpublished work: nothing
  identifiable leaves the host, and results are not pre-announced.
- `calendar` — scheduling a post around a talk or a deadline.

# How to propose

Give him, per item: the platform, the draft in his voice, the link it points to,
any image together with its alt text, and a suggested time — then let him choose
what goes out. Do not fan one message across every platform silently, and do not
mix a mechanical share (repost a collaborator, post the identifier of a new
release) with an editorial original post in the same batch — he will wave the
first through and want to read the second.
